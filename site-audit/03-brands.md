## Page: Discover Top Garage Door Brands
**URL:** https://trinitygaragedoorservice.com/brands/
**Brand category:** Hub
**Page type / role:** Brands hub (overview / index of all 10 brands)
**Purpose (1–2 sentences):** A landing/index page that frames brand choice as important and routes visitors to the 10 individual brand pages. It splits brands into two buckets — those the company installs and those it only services — and pushes estimate/repair CTAs.

### Copy
- **H1 / Hero headline:** "Discover Top Garage Door Brands"
- **Hero subtext / intro:** "Selecting the garage door for your home is a choice that is greatly impacted by the brand you choose. The ideal garage door not only fulfills a function but also adds to the visual charm of your house."
- **Section headings (in order):** "Discover Top Garage Door Brands" → "CHOOSING THE RIGHT GARAGE DOOR & OPENER BRAND IS CRUCIAL" → "Happy Customers" → "Hear What Our Satisfied Clients Have to Say" → "Brands we do Installation, Repair and Maintenance" → "Brands we Service, Repair and Maintain" → "Connect with Us" → "Get in Touch with Us Today!"
- **Body copy (brand blurbs + company relationship — verbatim):**
  - **Clopay (door):** "Clopay is a trusted name in the industry, renowned for its wide selection of stylish and durable garage doors." → framed as install + maintain.
  - **C.H.I. (door):** "C.H.I is renowned for its dedication to artisanship and creativity." → framed as repair.
  - **Hörmann (door):** "When it comes to premium garage doors, Hörmann is a brand that has set industry standards for quality, durability, and innovation." → framed as service.
  - **Amarr (door):** "When it comes to durable, stylish, and reliable garage doors, Amarr Garage Doors is a name that stands out in the industry." → framed as repair.

### Meaning & relationships
- **Why it exists:** SEO + trust signaling + routing. It collects recognizable manufacturer names to capture brand-name searches and to reassure visitors the company works with "top brands," then funnels them into 10 child pages and into estimate/repair conversion flows.
- **Copy + images:** Brand logos do the heavy lifting (visual trust). Body copy is thin per brand (one sentence each); the real depth lives on the child pages.
- **Relation to other pages:** This is the parent of all 10 brand detail pages (links to each). It also cross-links the full site nav (services, service areas, portfolio, blogs) and the two conversion pages (`/request-an-estimate/`, `/schedule-a-repair/`). The two footer buckets ("install" vs "service") are a recurring template element repeated on every brand page.

## Cross-page observations

**Shared template — yes, clearly.** All 10 brand detail pages run on one WordPress template with a fixed content arc: hero (H1 = "[Brand] Garage Door[s]/Openers" + an install/repair sub-line) → "[Brand]: [tagline]" intro → "A Brief History of [Brand]" → "Residential & Commercial ..." → a "Top-/Best-Selling [Brand] ..." list of 5 models (Wayne Dalton has 4) each prefixed by the same `checked-1.png` checkmark → "Why choose / Reasons" → "Key considerations / What to look for" → "Happy Customers" testimonials → the two brand-logo strips ("Brands we do Installation, Repair and Maintenance" / "Brands we Service, Repair and Maintain") → contact form (Name/Email/Message/Send). The header nav, footer brand strips, award badges (Angi Super Service 2024, "Elite," Ad Listing), three phone numbers, and the `Keep-Garage-Door-Openers-Out-of-Reach.png` safety graphic are identical sitewide.

**Door vs opener framing.** Door brands (Clopay, C.H.I., Hörmann, Amarr) showcase door *collections* (e.g., Clopay Canyon Ridge®/Coachman®/Gallery®; C.H.I. Carriage House; Amarr Heritage/Lincoln). Opener brands (Chamberlain, Craftsman, Genie, LiftMaster, Linear, Wayne Dalton) showcase *opener model numbers/HP/drive types* (belt/chain/wall-mount/jackshaft, smart features). The only structural tell between the two is door-collection names vs opener model SKUs; the surrounding template is otherwise word-for-word parallel.

**Substantial vs thin.** Moderately substantial in word count (each page has ~6–8 sections of generic brand history + 5 model blurbs), but **thin in originality** — the prose is boilerplate-generic and the per-page value is essentially the H1, the 5 model names, and 3 product photos. Clopay (5 named collections, 3 photos) and Amarr (richest heading set + a real Google-review customer avatar) are the fullest; Wayne Dalton is the thinnest (4 models, a reused hero image, a shared filler photo). Several "product" photos are AI/stock-style generic shots (Amarr's hero is a non-Amarr "garage-door" file; the `b53d7c79` photo and the safety PNG recur as filler on multiple brands).

**Key inconsistency — install vs service claims contradict the hub.** The hub sorts brands into "Brands we do Installation" (only Clopay + LiftMaster + one unlabeled `Image-12.png` logo) vs "Brands we Service" (Wayne Dalton, Chamberlain, Genie, Linear, etc.), and its per-brand blurbs call most brands repair-only. But **every brand detail page claims "installation, replacement, maintenance, and repair"** for its brand — including C.H.I., Hörmann, Amarr, Chamberlain, Craftsman, Genie, Linear, and Wayne Dalton, which the hub says are service-only. Only Clopay and LiftMaster are consistent across both. This door/opener install-vs-service story should be reconciled in the redesign (decide which brands Trinity genuinely installs/sells new vs only repairs, and say it once, consistently).

**SEO intent.** These are textbook brand-name landing pages — built to rank for "[brand] garage door[s]/opener [service area]" queries and to harvest brand trust. The dense internal linking (every page links all 10 brands + all services + both conversion pages) is a link-equity/SEO play. Accessibility is weak: virtually no `alt` text on product photos or logos (only C.H.I.'s footer logos and Amarr's one customer avatar carried alt).

**IA recommendations for the redesign:**
- Replace 10 near-duplicate thin pages with **one filterable Brands catalog** (filter by type: Doors vs Openers; and by relationship: Install/Sell vs Service-only). Keep lightweight per-brand anchors/detail panels for SEO, generated from structured data rather than hand-copied boilerplate.
- **Reconcile the install-vs-service claim once** in a single source of truth (a `relationship` field per brand) so the catalog, cards, and any detail view never contradict each other again.
- Model lists should be **structured data** (brand → model → type/HP/drive/photo), enabling reuse across catalog, detail, and any future "request a quote for this model" flow — and eliminating the duplicated checkmark-list markup.
- Fix accessibility: real, descriptive `alt` text for every product photo and brand logo; replace generic/AI stock door shots with genuine brand product imagery (or label them as illustrative).
- De-duplicate boilerplate: the two footer brand strips, badges, safety graphic, and three-county phone block belong in shared components, not re-pasted per page.
- Decide whether "brands" deserve top-nav prominence at all, or whether they're better as a supporting catalog under Services, with the primary CTAs ("Request an Estimate" / "Schedule a Repair") kept front-and-center as they already are.

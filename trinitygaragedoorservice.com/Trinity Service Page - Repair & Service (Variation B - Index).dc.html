Signs Something's Wrong

A Door Rarely Breaks Without Warning

Here are the things our customers tell us about most.

A loud bang from the garage, almost like a gunshot — often a spring letting go.

The door won't open at all, or it lifts a few inches and stops.

The opener hums or runs, but the door just sits there.

Grinding, squealing, or a rattle that keeps getting louder.

The door moves in a jerk, or one side rides higher than the other.

A gap at the bottom when the door is shut, or a panel that sags.

Some of these are small. Some are not. A door that's off balance or running on a tired spring puts a lot of strain on the rest of the system, so it pays to have someone look before a cheap fix turns into a big one.

What We Repair

A Handful of Parts That Take a Beating

When one part goes, the whole door stops cooperating. Here's what we work on.

01

Springs

The springs do the heavy lifting and fail most. We replace torsion and extension springs, balance the door, and test it — please don't DIY this one, the tension can cause serious injury.

02

Cables

Cables work with the springs to raise and lower the door. When one frays or snaps the door hangs crooked — we replace and reset them so it pulls evenly again.

03

Rollers

Worn rollers make a door loud and rough, and a seized one can pull it off track. We swap them out — the difference in noise is usually night and day.

04

Tracks

A bent or loose track makes the door bind, stick, or jump. We straighten what we can and replace what we can't.

05

Openers

Motor runs but the door won't move? Remote acting up? We repair openers, sensors, and wiring — chain, belt, screw, and smart units alike.

06

Hinges & Hardware

Loose hinges, bolts, and brackets are small parts that cause big rattles. We tighten and replace them so the door moves quietly again.

We service all the major brands, doors and openers both — Clopay, C.H.I., Hörmann, and Amarr doors, plus LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman, Linear, and Wayne Dalton openers. Not sure what you've got? No worries — we'll figure it out when we get there.

Twice a Year
Tune-Ups & Maintenance

The Cheapest Repair Is the One You Head Off Early

A yearly tune-up keeps a door running smooth and catches the small stuff before it strands you. When we service a door, we:

Lubricate the moving parts
Tighten the hardware
Check springs & cables for wear
Run a balance test
Test the safety sensors

This matters more here than up north. Our heat, humidity, and salt air are hard on metal — springs and cables rust and tire out sooner, rollers stiffen, and tracks pick up corrosion, especially near the coast. A little attention twice a year goes a long way in this climate.

A Few Questions We Hear A Lot

Frequently Asked

One of my springs broke. Can I still use the door?
Please don't. With a spring gone, the door is far heavier than the opener was built to lift, and running it can burn out the motor or bring the door down hard. Leave it shut and give us a call.
Can I replace a spring myself?
We'd really rather you didn't. Garage door springs hold a tremendous amount of tension, and they can hurt you badly if they let go while you're working on them. This is a job for someone with the right tools and the training to use them.
How long should my springs and opener last?
Springs usually run about seven to ten years, depending on how often you use the door and how well it's kept up. Openers tend to go ten to fifteen. Florida's climate can shorten both, so don't be surprised if yours wear a little sooner.
Do you charge for an estimate?
No. Estimates are free. We'll look at the door, tell you what it needs, and give you a price with no pressure to book on the spot.
How fast can you get here?
Often the same day. For emergencies we're around the clock, so call us and we'll tell you the soonest we can be there.

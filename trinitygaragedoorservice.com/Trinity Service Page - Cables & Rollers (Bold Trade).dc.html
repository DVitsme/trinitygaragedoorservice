The Short Version

The Small Parts That Do Quiet Work

A steel cable runs down each side of your door. They work with the springs to carry the weight, a few hundred pounds, and keep both sides moving together. The bottom of each cable hooks to a bracket at the lower corner, then winds around a drum at the end of the spring shaft.

Because the cables carry that load all the time, they sit under real tension, even when the door is just closed and quiet. That's why the bottom bracket and the cables are not something to poke at, it's a job for the right tools and training.

Signs A Cable Is Going

Before It Snaps

A failing cable usually shows itself before it snaps. If you see any of these, stop using the door.

Crooked Door

The door looks crooked, with one side lower than the other. A cable on one side has slipped or stretched.

Hanging Loose

A cable is hanging loose or coiled up beside the door instead of wound neatly on the drum.

Lifts Unevenly

The door lifts unevenly or feels like it's binding on one side as it moves.

Dust Or Shavings

Fine brown dust or metal shavings near the drum or down the track. That's a cable starting to fray.

A Snap, Then A Drop

You hear a snap, and the door drops or jams. A door running on a broken cable can pull itself off the track.

Stop Using It

A door running on a frayed or broken cable can drop or pull itself off the track. If you spot the signs, stop using it and give us a call.

What Your Rollers Do

And Why The Type Matters

Rollers are the little wheels that ride inside the track and carry the door. A typical door has around ten of them, and not all rollers are the same.

Steel

Cheap, But Loud

What a lot of builders use. They run steel against steel, so they're loud, they rust, and they tend to wear out in just a few years.

Nylon

Quieter, Longer Lasting

Quieter and smoother, they don't rust, and they usually last far longer than steel. A big step up for not much money.

Sealed
Bearing

The Best Of The Bunch

The bearings are packed and sealed against dirt and moisture, so they run quiet and smooth and basically take care of themselves. The biggest upgrade you can make.

Florida is hard on this hardware. Cables and roller bearings are steel, and steel and salt air don't get along. Near the coast, and in our humidity just about everywhere, cables rust and fray sooner and roller bearings seize up faster than they would up north. That's a big reason we like sealed bearing rollers and good hardware down here, they hold up a lot longer in this climate.

What We Do On The Visit

Worked Safely, All The Way Through

We lock out the opener and secure the door. Nothing can move while we work, since this hardware sits under spring tension.
We replace cables in matched pairs. If one is worn, the other is the same age and headed the same way. Doing both keeps the door balanced and saves a second trip.
We replace or upgrade the rollers. Usually all at once, since they wear together. Happy to put in nylon or sealed bearing rollers if you want the quiet.
We check the springs while we're in there. Cables, springs, and rollers wear on the same clock. If the springs are close to done, we'll show you, so you're not back next month.
We lubricate, realign, and test. We tighten the hardware, cycle the door, check the balance, and make sure the safety reverse works before we leave.

A garage door is one connected system, and the parts age together. We replace in pairs and check the springs so we fix it once and it holds. Estimates are free before any work starts.

Cable And Roller Questions

Frequently Asked

Can you just replace the one cable that broke?
We replace them in pairs. The other cable is the same age and just as worn, so doing both keeps the door balanced and saves you a second visit in a few weeks.
Are nylon or sealed bearing rollers worth the upgrade?
For most people, yes. They're much quieter, they last a lot longer, and sealed bearing rollers shrug off our humidity. It's one of the best small upgrades you can make to a door.
My door is really loud. Is that the rollers?
Often, yes. Worn steel rollers are a common cause of a noisy door. Dry hinges and loose hardware can add to it, and a tune up usually quiets all of it down.
Is it safe to use the door with a frayed cable?
We'd rather you didn't. A frayed cable can snap, and then the door drops or jumps the track. Leave it be and give us a call.
Can you come out the same day?
Usually. We keep cables and rollers stocked on the truck, so most of these are a one visit fix. Call (813) 279-6785 and we'll find you a slot.

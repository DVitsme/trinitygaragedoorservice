# DIY Garage Door Troubleshooting Guide

Your garage door plays a big role in your home's security and convenience. Some problems you can sort out yourself with a little know how. Others are best left to a professional. This guide helps you tell the difference, with simple fixes for the small stuff and honest advice on when to call us.

## A quick word on safety
A garage door is a complex system: springs, cables, rollers, tracks, and an opener, all working together under tension. Before you try any fix:

* **Cut the power.** Unplug the opener so it cannot start while you work.
* **Use the right tools.** Keep a screwdriver, a wrench, and a good garage door lubricant handy.
* **Wear eye protection and gloves.**
* **Leave the springs and cables alone.** They are under high tension and can hurt you badly. Do not touch them unless you are trained.

## Small problems you can usually fix yourself

### Noisy door
**What you notice:** grinding, squeaking, or rattling when the door runs.
**Try this:**
* Tighten any loose bolts and screws on the door and the tracks.
* Coat the hinges, rollers, and springs with a good garage door lubricant for smoother, quieter operation.
* Skip the plastic parts, since lube can damage them, and wipe the tracks clean rather than greasing them.

### Door won't close all the way
**What you notice:** the door starts down, then reverses before it reaches the floor.
**Try this:**
* Wipe the safety sensors near the bottom of the tracks with a soft cloth.
* Make sure both sensors line up and nothing is blocking the beam.
* Clear any dirt or debris in the door's path.

### Remote not working
**What you notice:** the door ignores the remote.
**Try this:**
* Put fresh batteries in the remote.
* Reprogram it using the manufacturer's steps.
* Get within range and make sure nothing is blocking the signal.

### Door opens or closes unevenly
**What you notice:** one side rides higher or lower than the other.
**Try this:**
* Check for debris in the tracks.
* Make sure the tracks are snug and aligned.
* Use a level to confirm.

### Door runs slowly
**What you notice:** the door moves slower than it used to.
**Try this:**
* Lubricate the rollers and hinges.
* Clear any debris from the tracks.
* Check the opener's speed setting if it has one.

## Bigger problems that need a pro
Some repairs carry real risk and call for a trained technician. If you run into any of these, leave it to us.

### Broken springs
**What you notice:** a loud bang, and the door is suddenly heavy and won't open.
**Leave it alone:** springs are under huge tension and are dangerous to handle. Call us for a safe replacement. Here's our [spring repair](/services/repair/spring/) page.

### Snapped or frayed cables
**What you notice:** the door hangs crooked or won't lift evenly.
**Leave it alone:** the cables connect to the high tension springs. Don't handle a broken one. See our [cable and roller repair](/services/repair/cables-rollers/) page.

### Opener runs but the door won't move
**What you notice:** the motor hums or runs, but nothing happens.
**Try first:** check that the door is still connected to the trolley, and make sure the opener has power.
**If that doesn't do it:** it's likely the opener, the sensors, or the wiring. See our [opener repair](/services/repair/opener/) page.

### Door off the track
**What you notice:** the door is crooked or has jumped out of the tracks.
**Leave it alone:** don't force it back on. A door off its track can fall. Call us to realign it safely. Here's our [off track repair](/services/repair/off-track/) page.

### Damaged panels or dents
**What you notice:** visible damage that affects how the door looks or works.
**Maybe DIY:** a small dent can sometimes be tapped out with a block of wood and a mallet. For anything more, a panel may need replacing.

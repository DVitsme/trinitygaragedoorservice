# Garage Door Spring Repair & Replacement

The spring is what does the heavy lifting on your garage door, and we mean that literally. It carries the weight of a door that can run a few hundred pounds, so when a spring goes, the whole door usually quits with it. You might hear a loud bang from the garage, find the door stuck shut, or watch it drop faster than it should. That's a job for a tech, not a Saturday project. We replace garage door springs all over Tampa Bay, often the same day you call.

## Signs your spring is going, or already gone

Springs usually drop a hint or two before they quit for good. Keep an eye out for these:

* **The door won't open.** The opener runs and strains, but the door barely lifts or won't move at all. A broken spring is the usual reason.
* **A loud bang from the garage.** People tell us it sounded like a gunshot or a firecracker. That's often a torsion spring snapping, even when nothing looked wrong a minute earlier.
* **A gap in the spring.** Walk out and look at the spring above the door. If you see a two inch gap where the coil separated, it's broken.
* **The door feels heavy or drops fast.** Lift it by hand. If it fights you the whole way or crashes down, the spring isn't carrying the weight anymore.
* **The door looks crooked.** One side hangs lower or the door rises at an angle. That points to a spring or cable problem on one side.

If any of that sounds familiar, stop using the door and give us a call. Running the opener against a broken spring can burn out the motor and bend the door panels.

## Torsion or extension: the two kinds of springs

Just about every door uses one of two setups, and it helps to know which one you've got.

**Torsion springs** sit on a metal shaft above the door, running parallel to the opening. As the door moves, the spring winds and unwinds, and that turning force runs through cables and drums on each end to raise and lower the door. Torsion is the more common setup on newer doors. It runs smoother, lasts longer, and handles a heavy door better.

**Extension springs** run along the horizontal tracks on each side of the door, up near the ceiling. They stretch as the door closes and pull back as it opens. You'll see these more on older or lighter doors. They cost less, but they wear out sooner, and a snapped extension spring can whip loose if there's no safety cable running through it.

Either way, the spring is what makes a heavy door feel light. Without a good one, your opener is just dragging dead weight, and that wears on every part of the system.

## Why springs wear out faster down here

Every spring has a rough lifespan, and it's measured in cycles, not years. One cycle is the door going up and then back down. A standard spring is built for around 10,000 cycles, so a household that runs the door a few times a day can wear one out in roughly seven to ten years. Use it more than that and the clock moves faster.

Florida piles on its own wear. Our humidity stays high most of the year, and damp air against bare steel means rust. Rust digs into the coils, adds friction, and weakens the metal, so a spring can give out well before it ever reaches its cycle count. Closer to the coast, salt in the air speeds that up even more. The heat and storm season don't do springs any favors either.

That's a big reason we talk with a lot of homeowners about high cycle springs. They're built to take more open and close cycles before they fail, which buys you years in a climate that's hard on hardware.

## Why this isn't a do it yourself fix

We're not saying that to drum up business. A garage door spring is wound under serious tension, enough stored energy to throw a door that weighs a few hundred pounds. When one lets go, or when someone unwinds it the wrong way, it can break fingers, kick a winding bar across the garage, or worse. People land in the ER over this every year.

It also takes the right tools and winding bars to set the tension correctly and balance the door afterward. A spring that's even a little off throws the whole door out of balance and grinds down your opener. We'd rather you stay clear and let a tech handle it. We do these every day.

## Spring repair FAQs

**My door won't open and the opener just hums. Is it the spring?**
Usually, yes. When a spring breaks, the opener is suddenly trying to lift the full weight of the door and can't. Don't keep hitting the button, because that can damage the motor. Call us and we'll take a look.

**Do I really need to replace both springs if only one broke?**
On a two spring door, we recommend it. Both springs have the same age and the same wear, so the second one is usually close behind. Doing both at once keeps the door balanced and saves you another trip charge down the road. On a single spring door, we just replace the one.

**How long do garage door springs last?**
Most run about seven to ten years, depending on how often you use the door. Since springs are rated in cycles, a busy household wears them out sooner. Our humidity and salt air can cut into that too.

**What are high cycle springs?**
They're springs built to handle more open and close cycles before they wear out. If you use your door a lot, or you just want longer gaps between repairs, they're worth asking about. We'll tell you whether they make sense for your door.

# Garage Door Cable & Roller Repair

Cables and rollers are the small parts that do a lot of quiet work. The cables help the springs lift the door evenly, and the rollers let it glide up and down the track. When they're in good shape, you never think about them. When they wear out, the door gets loud, rough, or crooked, and sometimes it stops moving altogether. We replace both, all over Tampa Bay, often the same day.

## What your cables do

Your garage door has a steel cable running down each side. They work with the springs to carry the weight of the door, which can be a few hundred pounds, and to keep both sides moving together. On most doors the bottom of each cable hooks to a bracket at the lower corner of the door, then runs up and winds around a drum at the end of the spring shaft.

Because the cables carry that load all the time, they sit under real tension, even when the door is just closed and quiet. That's why the bottom bracket and the cables are not something to poke at. We'll come back to that.

One more thing worth knowing. If your door uses extension springs, the long kind that run along the tracks near the ceiling, each spring should have a thin safety cable threaded through it. If that spring ever breaks, the safety cable keeps the pieces from flying across the garage. A lot of older homes around here are missing them, and we'll add them when we see they're gone.

## Signs a cable is going

A failing cable usually shows itself before it snaps:

* The door looks **crooked**, with one side lower than the other.
* A cable is **hanging loose** or coiled up beside the door.
* The door **lifts unevenly** or feels like it's binding on one side.
* You spot **fine brown dust or metal shavings** near the drum or down the track. That's a cable starting to fray.
* You hear a **snap**, and the door drops or jams.

If you see any of that, stop using the door. A door running on a frayed or broken cable can drop or pull itself off the track.

## What your rollers do, and why the type matters

Rollers are the little wheels that ride inside the track and carry the door as it moves. A typical door has around ten of them. They take a beating, and not all rollers are the same:

* **Steel rollers** are what a lot of builders use. They're cheap, but they run steel against steel, so they're loud, they rust, and they tend to wear out in just a few years.
* **Nylon rollers** are quieter and smoother, they don't rust, and they usually last far longer.
* **Sealed bearing rollers** are the best of the bunch. The bearings are packed and sealed against dirt and moisture, so they run quiet and smooth and basically take care of themselves. If you want the biggest upgrade, this is it.

If your door sounds like it belongs in a horror movie, worn steel rollers are often the reason, and switching to nylon or sealed bearing rollers is one of the cheapest ways to make a door feel new again.

## Signs a roller is going

* **Grinding, rumbling, or squealing** as the door moves.
* The door **hesitates or jumps** at the same spot every time.
* **Jerky or shaky** travel instead of a smooth glide.
* A roller that's **cracked, flat on one side, or out of the track.**

Worn rollers do more than make noise. They chew up the track and raise the odds the door jumps off it entirely, which turns a small fix into a bigger one. (If yours already came off, see our [off track repair](/services/repair/off-track/) page.)

## What we do on the visit

Cables and rollers sit inside a system that's under spring tension, so we work it safely and all the way through:

* **We lock out the opener and secure the door** so nothing can move while we work.
* **We replace cables in matched pairs.** If one is worn, the other is the same age and headed the same way. Doing both keeps the door balanced and saves you a second trip.
* **We replace or upgrade the rollers,** usually all of them at once, since they wear together. We're happy to put in nylon or sealed bearing rollers if you want the quiet.
* **We check the springs while we're in there.** Cables, springs, and rollers all wear on the same clock, and a tired spring puts extra strain on the cables. If the springs are close to done, we'll show you, so you're not back to square one next month. (Here's our [spring repair](/services/repair/spring/) page.)
* **We lubricate, realign the track, and tighten the hardware.**
* **We test it before we leave.** We cycle the door, check that it holds its balance, and make sure the safety reverse works.

## Why we replace in pairs and check the springs

We know that can sound like we're adding to the job. We're not. A garage door is one connected system, and the parts age together. Replacing a single cable next to a worn one, or new rollers next to tired springs, just means another service call soon. We'd rather fix it once and have it hold.

## Florida is hard on this hardware

Cables and roller bearings are steel, and steel and salt air don't get along. Near the coast, and in our humidity just about everywhere, cables rust and fray sooner and roller bearings seize up faster than they would up north. That's a big reason we like sealed bearing rollers and good hardware down here. They hold up a lot longer in this climate.

## Why this isn't a do it yourself fix

The cables and the bottom bracket carry the full tension of the door every minute of the day. Loosen the wrong thing and a cable can whip loose or the door can come down. Even if you got a new cable on, the wrong length or a bad wind on the drum leaves the door unbalanced and unsafe. This is a job for someone with the right tools and the training to use them. We do these every day.

## Cable and roller questions

**Can you just replace the one cable that broke?**
We replace them in pairs. The other cable is the same age and just as worn, so doing both keeps the door balanced and saves you a second visit in a few weeks.

**Are nylon or sealed bearing rollers worth the upgrade?**
For most people, yes. They're much quieter, they last a lot longer, and sealed bearing rollers shrug off our humidity. It's one of the best small upgrades you can make to a door.

**My door is really loud. Is that the rollers?**
Often, yes. Worn steel rollers are a common cause of a noisy door. Dry hinges and loose hardware can add to it, and a tune up usually quiets all of it down.

**Is it safe to use the door with a frayed cable?**
We'd rather you didn't. A frayed cable can snap, and then the door drops or jumps the track. Leave it be and give us a call.

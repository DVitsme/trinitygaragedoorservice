## Start with the material

The right door comes down to the look you want, your budget, and how much upkeep you're willing to do. Here's the honest rundown on the four materials we install.

**Steel.** The most common choice, and for good reason. It's strong, it doesn't cost a fortune, and it barely needs maintenance. You can get it insulated, painted, or pressed to look like wood grain. Thin, cheap steel can dent, so we'll point you to a gauge that holds up.

**Wood.** Nothing else looks quite like real wood. It's warm, it can be stained or built custom, and it suits certain homes beautifully. The trade off is upkeep. Florida sun and humidity are hard on wood, so it needs sealing and the occasional refinish to keep it from warping or rotting. If you love the look but not the work, read the composite note below.

**Aluminum and glass.** Light, rust proof, and modern. Big glass panels in an aluminum frame let daylight pour into the garage, which people love for shops, gyms, and clean contemporary fronts. Glass gives you less insulation and less privacy than a solid door, so it's a style call as much as a function one.

**Composite.** The wood look without the wood headaches. Composite doors give you real grain and color over a tougher core that shrugs off moisture, rot, and bugs. In our climate that's a genuine advantage, and it's why a lot of folks who want the wood look end up here.

## Then the style

Material is what the door is made of. Style is the face it shows the street.

**Carriage house** doors look like the old swing open barn doors, usually with decorative hardware and a row of windows up top. They suit traditional and craftsman homes. Clopay's Reserve Collection wood carriage doors are a favorite when someone wants that look done in real wood.

**Raised panel** is the classic rectangular look you see on most streets. Clean, easy to live with, and it fits almost any house.

**Modern and full view** doors lean on flat panels or big glass and aluminum for a sharp, current look. Great on newer builds and flat front contemporary homes.

We'll bring you options that actually fit your house instead of talking you into the priciest thing on the truck. That's not how we work.

## Built for Florida weather

A garage door in Tampa Bay has two jobs most people don't think about until later.

First, heat. An insulated door slows the heat coming through what is, on a lot of houses, the biggest uninsulated surface they own. If your garage shares a wall with the house, or you use it as a shop or a gym, an insulated door keeps it more livable and takes some load off the AC. Insulation is rated by R value, and a higher number means more resistance to heat. Foam filled doors run quieter too, since the panel is more solid.

Second, wind. Florida code sets a wind rating for garage doors based on where your house sits, and the garage is usually the largest opening on the place. If it blows in during a storm, wind gets inside and pushes up on the roof, and that's how the serious damage starts. A wind rated door is built heavier and anchored properly so the opening holds. Most door swaps around here need a permit that confirms the door is rated for your address, and we handle that paperwork as part of the job.

## How the install goes

No surprises. Here's the order of events.

1. **Free estimate and consult.** We come look at the opening, talk through what you want, and give you a real number. No pressure to decide on the spot.
2. **Measure.** We measure the opening, the headroom, and the side room so the door and tracks fit right. New construction gets checked against the framing.
3. **Out with the old.** If there's an existing door, we take it down and haul it off. You don't deal with the old one.
4. **Install.** We set the tracks, panels, springs, and hardware, then mount or reconnect the opener.
5. **Test and clean up.** We balance the door, test the safety reverse and the opener, and run it a few times to be sure it's quiet and smooth. Then we clean up and take the mess with us.

Most single door installs wrap up in a day. We'll give you a straight timeline when we quote it.

## What a new door does for the house

The garage door is often the biggest thing on the front of a house. Swap a dented, faded door for a clean new one and the whole place reads differently from the street. It's also one of the few upgrades that tends to pay you back at resale, which is why people fixing up a house to sell often start here.

And you get the practical wins on top of the looks: better insulation, quieter operation, modern safety sensors, and an opener you can run from your phone if you want it.

## FAQs

**Do you install on new construction, or only replacements?**
Both. We do new builds and replacements all the time. On new construction we check the door against your framing; on a replacement we take down and haul off the old door.

**How long does an install take?**
Most single doors go in within a day. Bigger, custom, or double doors can take longer. We'll tell you the timeline when we give you the estimate, not after.

**Do I need a permit?**
In most of Tampa Bay, a door install or replacement needs a permit, and it confirms the door meets the wind rating for your area. We pull it and handle the inspection so you don't have to.

**Will a new door come with an opener?**
The opener is separate from the door. If yours still works well, we can reconnect it. If it's old or you want smart features, we can install a new one and set it up.

**Can you match a specific look I have in mind?**
Usually, yes. Between steel, wood, aluminum and glass, and composite, and the styles each brand offers, there's a lot of room. Show us a photo and we'll find something close.

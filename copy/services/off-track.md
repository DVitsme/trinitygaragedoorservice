# Off Track Repair & Replacement

It sits crooked, one side hangs lower than the other, a roller or two has popped out of the metal track, and the whole door looks like it's leaning into the garage. Sometimes it's stuck wide open. ## Why a garage door comes off track

A door rides up and down on a roller and track system, and it stays put as long as everything is lined up and under the right tension. A few things can throw it off:

* **A broken or frayed cable.** The cables keep the door balanced as it moves. When one snaps or slips off its drum, that side drops and the door twists out of the track.
* **Worn or broken rollers.** Rollers that are cracked, rusted, or coming apart don't sit in the track the way they should, and eventually one jumps out.
* **A bent or loose track.** If the track gets knocked out of shape, or the brackets holding it work loose, the rollers have nowhere good to go.
* **An impact.** This one is common. Somebody backs into the door, or a ladder or trailer clips it, and the track bends just enough to derail the door.
* **A broken spring.** When a spring lets go, the door can come down hard and uneven, and that sudden jolt is often enough to knock it off track.
* **Junk in the track.** Dirt, a stray bolt, even a buildup of old grease can block a roller and force it off its path.

Down here, salt air and humidity speed a lot of this up. Rollers seize and tracks corrode faster near the water, and a stiff, rusty system is a lot more likely to bind and jump.

## Please don't force it

We get why the instinct is to shove it back into place or keep tapping the opener until something happens. Please don't. An off track door is heavy and unbalanced, and it isn't sitting where it's supposed to be.

Hit the opener and you can drag the door further off, bend the track worse, snap a cable, or bring the whole thing down. Try to muscle it by hand and you're standing under a few hundred pounds that aren't held the way they should be. Leave it where it is, keep kids and cars clear of it, and call us. We do this safely every day, and it's a lot cheaper than fixing a door that fell.

## What we do on the visit

When we get there, we don't just pop the rollers back in and call it done. That fixes the symptom and skips the cause. Here's how we work it:

* **We find out why it happened.** The technician checks the rollers, cables, springs, track, and hardware to see what actually let go.
* **We get it realigned.** If the track is sound, we reset the rollers, straighten the door, and bring it back into line.
* **We replace what's worn or broken.** Bad rollers, a frayed cable, a damaged section of track, we swap out whatever caused the trouble so it doesn't happen again next week.
* **We reset the balance.** A door that comes off track is almost always out of balance. We get the springs and cables pulling evenly again.
* **We test the whole thing.** Before we leave, we run the door up and down, check that it holds in place on its own, and make sure the safety sensors reverse it the way they should.

## When you need a new track

A lot of off track doors just need a realign and a few new parts. But not always. If a track is badly bent, kinked, crushed, or rusted through, straightening it won't hold, and we'll tell you straight. In that case a full track replacement is the honest fix. New track means the door runs true again and you're not back to the same problem in a month.

We'll always walk you through what we're seeing and leave the choice with you. If a repair will do, we'll repair it. We're not going to sell you a new track you don't need.

## Questions we hear about off track doors

**My door is off track. Is it safe to use until you get here?**
No. Don't run the opener, and don't try to open it by hand. Every time it moves, an off track door can do more damage to itself or to whatever is underneath it. Leave it be and we'll handle it.

**Can I just put the roller back in myself?**
We really don't recommend it. The door is heavy and the springs are under high tension, and a door that drops while you're working on it can hurt you. Even if you got the roller back in, whatever knocked it out is still there.

**Do I need a whole new track, or can it be repaired?**
Most of the time it's a repair. We can realign a track that's still in good shape and replace the worn rollers or cable that caused the trouble. We only point you toward a new track when the old one is too bent or corroded to trust, and we'll show you which one you're dealing with.

**Can you get my car out if it's trapped inside?**
Usually, yes. Tell us when you call and we'll come ready to open the door safely so you can get your vehicle out.

**How long does the repair take?**
Many off track doors are back in service in a single visit. If it needs a part we have to grab, we'll let you know up front and get it sorted as quick as we can.

# Garage Door Tune Up & Maintenance

Nobody thinks about a garage door that's working right. A tune up is how you keep it that way. It's the heaviest moving thing in most homes, and it runs thousands of times a year, so a little regular upkeep keeps it quiet and reliable and heads off the breakdowns before they strand you with your car inside. We tune up doors all over Tampa Bay.

## What's in a Trinity tune up

When we service a door, we go through the whole system, not only the squeaky part:

* **Lubricate the moving parts.** Rollers, hinges, springs, bearings, and the opener's drive, all with the right garage door lubricant.
* **Tighten the hardware.** All that movement shakes bolts and brackets loose over time. We snug up the hinges, track brackets, and opener mounts.
* **Check the springs and balance.** We look the springs over for wear and rust, then test that the door is balanced and not leaning on the opener to do the springs' job.
* **Inspect the cables, rollers, and hinges.** We catch fraying cables, worn rollers, and loose hinges while they're still cheap to deal with.
* **Test the safety systems.** This is the big one, and it gets its own section below.
* **Check the opener.** We make sure the force and travel settings are right, so the door closes all the way and reverses when it should without slamming.
* **Look over the tracks and weather seal.** We check the track for dents and alignment and the bottom seal for cracks and gaps that let in water, bugs, and heat.

## The two safety systems we always test

Your opener has two separate safety features, and a lot of people don't realize they're different. Both have been required on openers since the early 1990s, and both need to work:

* **The photo eyes.** Those little sensors near the floor send an invisible beam across the opening. Break the beam while the door is closing, with a foot, a bag, or a pet, and the door should stop and head back up.
* **The auto reverse.** This is the opener's sense of touch. If the door meets something solid on the way down, it should reverse on its own, even if nothing crossed the beam.

We test both every time. If a door doesn't reverse the way it should, that's not a someday problem, it's a fix it now problem, especially in a house with kids or pets.

## Why upkeep matters more in Florida

Our climate is rough on a garage door. Heat breaks down cheap lubricant faster, so parts run dry and wear. Humidity rusts springs, cables, and bearings. And near the coast, salt air speeds all of it up. A door that might cruise along on once a year service up north often wants a look twice a year down here, especially close to the water. The good news is that a little attention goes a long way, and a well kept door lasts many years longer than a neglected one.

## How often should you do it

For a normal household, once a year is a good rhythm. If you run the door constantly, a busy family in and out all day, or you live near the coast, twice a year is smarter. Between visits, a quick monthly look on your own helps a lot, which brings us to the stuff you can do yourself.

## A few things you can do yourself

We're not trying to keep secrets. Here's honest homeowner maintenance you can handle without us:

* **The balance test.** With the door closed, pull the manual release, then lift the door by hand to about waist or chest height and let go. A healthy door mostly stays put. If it slides down or drifts up, the springs are out of balance and it's time to call. Don't keep running a door that won't hold.
* **The safety test.** Lay a scrap of wood flat in the doorway, a piece of two by four is perfect, and close the door. It should touch the wood and reverse within a second or two. Then wave something through the photo eye beam while it closes and watch it reverse.
* **Light lubrication.** A little garage door lube on the rollers, hinges, and springs every few months keeps things quiet. Two things to skip: don't use a spray oil like WD 40, since it dries out and attracts grit, and don't grease the tracks, just wipe them clean. The rollers ride the track, they don't need it slick.

If any of those checks feel off, that's when to call us.

## Tune up questions

**How often should my garage door be serviced?**
Once a year for most homes, twice if you use the door a lot or live near the coast. A quick monthly check on your own helps between visits.

**What's the point if nothing's broken?**
That's exactly the point. A tune up keeps things from breaking, and it keeps the safety features working. It's a lot cheaper than an emergency call when a worn part finally gives out.

**Can a tune up fix my broken spring?**
No. A broken spring is a repair, not maintenance. We can replace it on the same trip, though. See our [spring repair](/services/repair/spring/) page, or our [repair and service](/services/repair/) page for everything else.

**Can I just maintain it myself?**
You can handle the basics, and we'll show you how. The lubrication, the balance test, and the safety test are all fair game. The spring tension and cable work are where you'll want a tech, since those parts are under heavy load.

**Do you service doors you didn't install?**
Of course. We tune up any major brand of door and opener, no matter who put it in.

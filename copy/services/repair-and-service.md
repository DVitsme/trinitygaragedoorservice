## When your garage door quits on you

## Signs something's wrong

A garage door rarely breaks without warning. Here are the things our customers tell us about most:

* A loud bang from the garage, almost like a gunshot. That's often a spring letting go.
* The door won't open at all, or it lifts a few inches and stops.
* The opener hums or runs, but the door just sits there.
* Grinding, squealing, or a rattle that keeps getting louder.
* The door moves in a jerk, or one side rides higher than the other.
* A gap at the bottom when the door is shut, or a panel that sags.

Some of these are small. Some are not. A door that's off balance or running on a tired spring puts a lot of strain on the rest of the system, so it pays to have someone look before a cheap fix turns into a big one.

## What we repair

A garage door is a simple machine with a handful of parts that take a beating. When one goes, the whole door stops cooperating. Here's what we work on:

**Springs.** The springs do the heavy lifting, and they're the part that fails most. We replace both torsion and extension springs, balance the door, and test it before we leave. This is the one repair we really don't want you trying yourself. A garage door spring holds enough tension to do serious damage if it slips.

**Cables.** The cables work with the springs to raise and lower the door. When one frays or snaps, the door can hang crooked or drop on one side. We replace cables and reset them so the door pulls evenly again.

**Rollers.** Worn rollers make a door loud and rough, and a cracked or seized roller can pull the door right off its track. We swap them out, and the difference in noise is usually night and day.

**Tracks.** A bent or loose track makes the door bind, stick, or jump. We straighten what we can and replace what we can't.

**Openers.** Sometimes the motor runs and the door won't move. Sometimes the remote only works when it feels like it. The trouble is usually the opener, the sensors, or the wiring, and we repair all of it. We fix openers that run on a chain, a belt, or a screw, and we handle the smart units too.

**Hinges and hardware.** Loose hinges, bolts, and brackets are small parts that cause big rattles. We tighten and replace them so the door moves quietly again.

We service all the major brands, doors and openers both. That covers Clopay, C.H.I., Hormann, and Amarr doors, plus LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, Craftsman, Linear, and Wayne Dalton openers. If you're not sure what you've got, no worries. We'll figure it out when we get there.

## Tune ups and maintenance

The cheapest repair is the one you head off early. A yearly tune up keeps a door running smooth and catches the small stuff before it strands you. When we service a door, we lubricate the moving parts, tighten the hardware, check the springs and cables for wear, run a balance test, and make sure the safety sensors stop the door the way they should.

This matters more here than it does up north. Our heat, humidity, and salt air are hard on metal. Springs and cables rust and tire out sooner, rollers stiffen up, and tracks pick up corrosion, especially closer to the coast. A little attention twice a year goes a long way in this climate.

## A few questions we hear a lot

**One of my springs broke. Can I still use the door?**
Please don't. With a spring gone, the door is far heavier than the opener was built to lift, and running it can burn out the motor or bring the door down hard. Leave it shut and give us a call.

**Can I replace a spring myself?**
We'd really rather you didn't. Garage door springs hold a tremendous amount of tension, and they can hurt you badly if they let go while you're working on them. This is a job for someone with the right tools and the training to use them.

**How long should my springs and opener last?**
Springs usually run about seven to ten years, depending on how often you use the door and how well it's kept up. Openers tend to go ten to fifteen. Florida's climate can shorten both, so don't be surprised if yours wear a little sooner.
